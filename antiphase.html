How noise cancelling works

1Listen

A tiny microphone on the headphones hears the noise outside: engines, air-con, chatter.

2Flip

A chip builds the mirror image of that wave in a fraction of a millisecond. Every peak becomes a valley.

3Cancel

The speaker plays the flipped wave. Peaks meet valleys, the sum is flat, and the noise is gone. Your music was never in the mic signal, so it passes through untouched.

Why it isn’t magic

  • Speed. The chip has to react before the sound reaches your ear. Slow it down and high-pitched noise is the first to escape. Try the Chip delay slider.
  • Accuracy. The mirror must match in loudness and timing. If it’s off, you only get partial cancelling, none, or even more noise.
  • Predictability. Steady, low sounds (engines, air-con) cancel best. A sudden bark or clap is over before the chip can answer.
